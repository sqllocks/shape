## Interpretations (for the lead)

1. **`kept_because`** lists the content ids of entries *older than the cutoff* that were kept, under
   every reason that applies (an entry can be in several lists); entries at or after the cutoff are
   kept because they are new and are not listed. `entries_removed` and `entries_kept` are counts.
2. **A ref or tag pointing at a content id keeps every entry with that content id** (the issue's
   "every entry whose content id is pointed at"), so `latest` also keeps an old entry of the same
   bytes.
3. **`before` as a string**: a UTC ISO 8601 timestamp with `Z` or an offset (converted), or a date
   `YYYY-MM-DD` read as midnight UTC. A timestamp without a zone is refused like a naive datetime.
   A non-string, non-datetime is `TypeError`; so is a `keep_last` that is not an int (bool
   included). `names=[]` is `ValueError`.
4. **Unreadable log lines** and entries without `created_at` are kept verbatim and reported under
   `skipped` as `logs/<name>.jsonl:<line>`; `keep_last` counts the readable, timed entries.
5. **Orphans**: an object nothing points at is removed whatever its age, also when it was orphaned
   before (that is how a second prune finishes an interrupted one).
6. **The lock** is a new `ROOT/prune.lock` (O_EXCL) held by a real prune (a dry run takes no lock).
   Commits had no lock before; `commit`, `tag` and `promote` now write a short-lived
   `ROOT/.commit-<uuid>.lock` *before* checking for `prune.lock`, and a prune takes `prune.lock`
   *before* waiting (up to 30 s) for those markers to go, so a commit that started just before a
   prune cannot interleave with it. A killed process can leave either file; the error names it.
7. Commit times now come from one clock read (`created_at` and `created` agree exactly); `created`
   keeps its format.
